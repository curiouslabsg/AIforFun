Speed and velocity

Done

Distance is how far you actually travel along your path. Displacement is the straight-line distance from start to finish, with a direction.

Speed is distance travelled per unit time. Velocity is speed in a stated direction: the rate of change of displacement.

average speed = total distance / total time

03

Graph detective

Done

First check which graph it is. The same shape means different things on each.

Displacement–time: the gradient is the velocity. Flat means at rest. Straight and sloping means uniform velocity. Curving means the velocity is changing.

Velocity–time: the gradient is the acceleration. Flat means uniform velocity (or at rest, if it sits on zero). A straight slope means uniform acceleration. A curve means non-uniform acceleration.

Classic trap: a horizontal line on a v–t graph is not “at rest” unless it is on v = 0.

Area under a velocity–time graph

Done

The area under a v–t graph is the displacement. Split it into rectangles and triangles, work out each one, then add them up.

triangle = ½ × base × height

The gradient of each straight section is its acceleration. A negative gradient is a deceleration.

Units check: (m/s) × s = m. If your area comes out in m/s, you have used the wrong axis.

05

Free fall and terminal velocity

Done

Near the Earth, every object falling without air resistance speeds up by about 10 m/s every second. That is the acceleration of free fall, g ≈ 10 m/s², the same for a feather and a hammer.

With air, the faster you fall, the bigger the air resistance. It grows until it equals your weight. Then the resultant force is zero, the acceleration is zero, and you fall at a steady terminal velocity.

Open the parachute and the air resistance suddenly jumps above your weight, so you decelerate until you reach a new, much lower terminal velocity.

Mission: jump, reach terminal velocity, then open the parachute and fall steadily at the new, safe speed.
