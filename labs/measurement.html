Physical quantities and SI units

Done

A physical quantity has a numerical magnitude and a unit. “The corridor is 12 long” tells you nothing. 12 m, 12 cm and 12 km are three very different corridors.

Everything in physics is built from six base quantities. Units like the newton or the joule are combinations of these.

Watch out: temperature’s SI unit is the kelvin (K), not °C. And mass is the kilogram, the only base unit with a prefix built in.

02

Prefixes

Done

Prefixes stop us writing lots of zeros. Each one is a power of ten stuck in front of the unit.

To convert to the base unit, replace the prefix with its power of ten: 450 µm = 450 × 10⁻⁶ m = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ m.

Common slip: m is milli (10⁻³) but M is mega (10⁶). Capital letters matter.

Orders of magnitude

Done

The order of magnitude is the power of ten nearest to a size. A person is about 10⁰ m, a hair is about 10⁻⁴ m wide, and the Earth is about 10⁷ m across. That is a range of 17 powers of ten from an atom to the Earth.

Vernier caliper

Read the main scale just left of the vernier’s zero. Then find the one vernier mark that lines up exactly with a main-scale mark. Each vernier division adds 0.01 cm.

reading = main scale + (mark × 0.01 cm)
Zero error: close the jaws first. If the vernier zero is to the right of the main zero, the zero error is positive. Corrected reading = reading − zero error.

cm

Correct: 0 / 3

Micrometer screw gauge

Read the sleeve first: whole millimetres above the line, and a half-millimetre mark below it if one is showing past the thimble. Then add the thimble mark that sits on the datum line, × 0.01 mm.

reading = sleeve + (thimble × 0.01 mm)
Easy mark to lose: a visible half-millimetre mark below the line adds 0.50 mm. Check for it every time.

Always turn the ratchet, not the thimble, so the wire is gripped with the same small force every time.

05

Timing short intervals

Done

A pendulum’s period is the time for one complete oscillation: there and back again. It is usually under 2 s, and your reaction time on a stopwatch is about 0.2 to 0.3 s. That error is huge compared with one period.

The fix: time 20 oscillations, then divide by 20. The reaction-time error is shared out over all 20, so it becomes 20 times smaller.

Practical tip: start and stop the watch as the bob passes the centre (the fiducial marker), where it moves fastest and the moment is easiest to judge.

Adding two vectors

Draw them tip to tail to scale. The resultant goes from the tail of the first to the tip of the second. Two 5 N forces can add up to anything from 0 N to 10 N, depending on the angle between them.
